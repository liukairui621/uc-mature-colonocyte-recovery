# Longitudinal bulk and single-cell transcriptomic reanalysis characterizes mucosal healing-associated recovery of a mature absorptive colonocyte score in ulcerative colitis

Kairui Liu, Rong Chen, Yipei Huang and Youxing Huang*

## Abstract

**Background:** Loss and reconstitution of mature absorptive epithelium accompany ulcerative colitis activity and mucosal healing, but bulk-biopsy signals cannot distinguish changes in epithelial composition from expression changes within a defined epithelial state. We tested whether a compact mature-colonocyte score tracked treatment outcome across longitudinal public cohorts and used single-cell data to resolve the source of the signal.

**Results:** We reanalysed four public datasets using a fixed equal-weight score of AQP8, HMGCS2, GUCA2A, CA2, SLC26A3 and MS4A12. In exploratory GSE92415, score change was associated with week-6 clinical response (65 paired patients; beta=0.599, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.148 to 1.051; P=0.010), but the internally specified primary test in GSE23597 did not meet its support criterion (32 pairs; beta=0.401, 95% CI -0.481 to 1.282; P=0.359). In GSE73661, the score increased more with infliximab-associated endoscopic healing (23 pairs; beta=1.195, 95% CI 0.503 to 1.887; P=0.00185); vedolizumab estimates were positive but imprecise. In GSE282122, remitters had lower baseline score within author-labelled non-ileal CT colonocytes than non-remitters (difference=-2.042; P=0.015) and a larger unadjusted longitudinal increase (16 evaluable patients; beta=2.556, 95% CI 1.267 to 3.845; exact P=0.00050), whereas follow-up levels did not clearly differ (difference=0.514; P=0.426). Baseline-adjusted ANCOVA attenuated the remission association (beta=1.442, HC3 95% CI 0.121 to 2.764; P=0.0347), and the v3.1-only estimate was imprecise (beta=1.577, 95% CI -0.765 to 3.918). Candidate-score change correlated with concurrent inflammatory-score change (r=-0.594; P=0.015). The CT-colonocyte proportion within epithelium showed no remission-associated increase, and algebraic decomposition assigned the larger group difference to within-group expression. Published-label DecontX retained the CT association across a fixed 15-state family (q=0.0169).

**Conclusions:** The six-gene score marks recovery of mature absorptive transcription in ulcerative colitis mucosa. Its larger rebound within author-labelled CT colonocytes in remitters was not explained by a greater captured CT fraction, but was partly dependent on lower baseline values and coincided with inflammation resolution. The score is a tissue-state correlate rather than a validated response predictor or independent mechanism.

**Keywords:** ulcerative colitis; mucosal healing; colonocyte; biologic therapy; longitudinal transcriptomics; single-cell RNA sequencing; pseudobulk

## Background

Mucosal healing is a central treatment target in ulcerative colitis (UC) because endoscopic improvement is more closely related to durable disease control than symptom relief alone [1,2]. Healing involves resolution of inflammatory injury and reconstruction of epithelial functions such as ion transport, water handling and barrier maintenance. Single-cell atlases have established that active UC is accompanied by loss or remodeling of mature absorptive colonocytes and emergence of inflammatory epithelial states [3,4]. These observations make mature epithelial expression a plausible molecular readout of mucosal recovery, but they also create an interpretation problem: a bulk-biopsy signal may increase because mature cells become more abundant, because a defined cell state changes its transcription, or because both occur.

Longitudinal mucosal transcriptomic studies of biologic therapy provide an opportunity to test outcome-linked change within the same patient. Public studies have profiled golimumab, infliximab, vedolizumab and adalimumab treatment using different platforms and clinical endpoints [5-9]. Previous work has identified inflammatory-response, permeability, epithelial and metabolism-related signatures associated with disease activity or treatment outcome [6,7,10,11]. The presence of those established programs means that another gene score is useful only if its evidence role is explicit and its relationship to composition, inflammation and existing epithelial biology is tested.

We assembled a compact six-gene score comprising AQP8, HMGCS2, GUCA2A, CA2, SLC26A3 and MS4A12 after transcriptome-wide and Hallmark exploration of a golimumab dataset and before patient-level benchmarking of 33 candidate and reference programs. These genes are established markers and effectors of mature absorptive colonocytes; the set was not intended to define a new cell type or an optimized predictor. We then fixed the gene membership, equal weights, annotation rules and one primary validation model before accessing the designated validation expression matrix. We retained the failed primary validation, evaluated endoscopic healing in infliximab and vedolizumab samples, and used an independent longitudinal single-cell cohort to separate captured cell composition from expression change within published epithelial states. The objective was to determine what biological component the score measures during treatment-associated recovery and to define the limits of its reproducibility.

## Methods

### Study design and public datasets

This was a secondary analysis of four public longitudinal transcriptomic datasets. GSE92415 was the exploratory discovery cohort and contained paired baseline and week-6 colonic biopsies from the PURSUIT-SC golimumab trial. GSE23597 was designated as the primary external clinical-response validation cohort and contained paired baseline and week-8 biopsies from placebo and infliximab arms. GSE73661 supplied a separate infliximab endoscopic-healing cohort and vedolizumab extensions at weeks 6 and 12. GSE282122, with processed counts archived at Zenodo record 14007626, was used to distinguish epithelial composition from within-state expression during adalimumab treatment [5-9]. All data were openly accessible and de-identified; no controlled-access data or newly collected samples were used. Dataset roles, platforms and retained sample numbers are shown in Table 1.

### Candidate score and annotation harmonization

The candidate score was the equal-weight arithmetic mean of log2-scale expression for AQP8, HMGCS2, GUCA2A, CA2, SLC26A3 and MS4A12. It was proposed post hoc after broad exploratory review of GSE92415 and before the 33-program patient-level comparison; no gene, direction or weight was fitted in a validation cohort. Before GSE23597 expression access, the genes, weights, outcome model and reporting rule were fixed in an internal specification. The candidate shared AQP8, GUCA2A, SLC26A3 and MS4A12 with a published 20-gene CT-colonocyte marker panel. Gene symbols were canonicalized using HGNC approved, previous and alias symbols, with approved exact matches prioritized and ambiguous aliases excluded [14]. Probe-level measurements mapping uniquely to the same canonical gene were averaged. All six candidate genes were present on the three microarray platforms.

To apply a common inflammatory adjustment across platforms, we started from the 200-gene Hallmark Inflammatory Response set [13] and fixed the 194 genes uniquely mapped and measurable on GPL13158, GPL570 and GPL6244 after alias harmonization. Patient-level change was calculated as follow-up minus baseline. Reference scores based on the published CT-colonocyte marker proxy, a 13-gene JAK/STAT program and Hallmark oxidative phosphorylation were examined descriptively; they were not treated as independent validation targets or direct competitors on a common scale. In discovery data, the candidate and published CT-colonocyte score changes were highly correlated (r=0.929), so no superiority or independence claim was made.

### Bulk-transcriptome analyses

GSE92415 deposited ArrayStudio RMA log2 expression and GSE73661 deposited aroma.affymetrix RMA log2 expression were retained without a second transformation. GSE23597 was deposited after GCOS 1.4 global scaling to 500 and was transformed as log2(max(expression, 1)) under the rule fixed before expression access. For each platform, uniquely mapped probes were averaged on the log2 scale after HGNC harmonization. No expression-based sample exclusion was applied. Paired exploratory transcriptome-wide changes were estimated with limma and Hallmark programs were screened with camera [15,16].

In GSE92415, the principal exploratory model was score change as a function of clinical response, treatment arm, baseline candidate score and change in the common inflammatory score. Sixty-five paired patients were retained. Models omitting the candidate baseline, adding baseline Mayo score, and adding published program covariates were treated as sensitivity or descriptive analyses.

The GSE23597 primary model was fixed as score change regressed on week-8 clinical response, treatment dose, baseline candidate score and inflammatory-score change. The prespecified support rule required a positive response coefficient and a two-sided ordinary least-squares P value below 0.05; the estimate, 95% confidence interval (CI) and exact P value were reported regardless of outcome. The final analysis included 32 independent patient pairs after one patient with unresolved duplicate baseline arrays was excluded according to the locked identity rule. A no-baseline model, HC3 robust intervals, infliximab-only analysis and leave-one-patient-out analyses were mandatory sensitivities. The internal freeze preceded validation expression access, but it was not a public preregistration.

For GSE73661, the outcome was endoscopic healing defined by the released Mayo endoscopic subscore. The adjusted support model regressed score change on healing, baseline score and inflammatory-score change. The infliximab analysis included 23 paired patients sampled at baseline and week 4 or 6. Vedolizumab extensions evaluated 27 baseline-to-week-6 pairs and a separate set of 13 baseline-to-week-12 pairs. The vedolizumab matrix and sample-level scores were already accessible before this extension; only the patient-level outcome analysis rules were newly fixed. These comparisons were supportive and were not allowed to replace or pool P values with the GSE23597 primary test.

### Single-cell composition and state analyses

We used corrected raw counts and released cell-state annotations for GSE282122 and did not recluster cells or define a candidate-high state [9]. In the source study, Cell Ranger 3.1.0 aligned reads to GRCh38-3.0.0; Scrublet-predicted doublets, cells with fewer than 500 detected genes, cells with more than 60% mitochondrial counts and genes detected in fewer than three cells were removed before BBKNN-supported clustering and marker-based annotation. Our main analysis additionally excluded cells carrying the released predicted-doublet flag. UC remission followed the source definition: at least two of SSCAI <=2, UCEIS <=1 and Nancy histological index <=1 at follow-up; escalation to another advanced biologic for uncontrolled activity was classified as non-remission.

The analysis was restricted to UC colonic or rectal biopsies with same-site pre-treatment and post-treatment observations. The resulting design comprised 41 site-matched pairs from 19 patients (6 in remission and 13 not in remission) and 378,575 annotated cells. Patient, rather than cell, was the unit of inference [17].

Two axes were specified. Composition was the change in the empirical-logit proportion of non-ileal CT colonocytes among non-ileal epithelial cells. State expression was obtained by summing raw counts within each sample-by-state stratum, normalizing by the corresponding total pseudobulk library, calculating the mean candidate-gene log counts per million, and taking the patient-level post-treatment minus pre-treatment difference. The primary CT-state analysis required at least 20 cells at each visit and retained 16 patients (6 remission, 10 non-remission); thresholds of 10 and 50 cells were sensitivity analyses.

For patients evaluable on both axes, a symmetric Kitagawa decomposition separated the difference in total linear-scale epithelial candidate expression into a CT-versus-other-epithelium composition component and a within-group expression component [19]. This is an algebraic description and was not interpreted as causal mediation.

### Ambient RNA and epithelial-state sensitivity analyses

DecontX was applied independently to each sample's raw counts using released cell-state labels as the cluster variable z, maxIter=500, estimated delta, convergence=0.001 and 5,000 variable genes [18]. Before running that sensitivity analysis, an interpretation rule specified that retention of the CT result alone would support a CT-restricted association and would withdraw any cross-lineage claim. Corrected candidate scores were analysed across a fixed family of 15 non-ileal epithelial states; states without sufficient paired patients were retained in the multiplicity denominator. Ten of 15 states were evaluable. PTPRC and COL1A1 were fixed as negative-control transcripts in raw CT-colonocyte pseudobulk, and patient-level changes in DecontX contamination summaries were also reported.

### Statistical analysis and reproducibility

Linear models used two-sided tests and 95% CIs. Ordinary least-squares results were primary for the bulk models; HC3 intervals and leave-one-patient-out results evaluated small-sample influence. The original single-cell group contrasts used ordinary least squares, HC3 intervals and exhaustive patient-label permutations; Benjamini-Hochberg correction covered the two named axes and the fixed 15-state epithelial family [20]. Because all remitters used 10x v3.1 and all v3 patients were non-remitters, an unrestricted chemistry-stratified remission permutation was not identifiable; the v3.1-only branch was therefore reported as the technical sensitivity.

After the initial single-cell result, a corrective analysis summarized baseline, follow-up and change on the same eligible patient-site pairs. Baseline-adjusted association was estimated by ANCOVA (follow-up score as outcome; remission and baseline score as predictors) with HC3 inference using residual t degrees of freedom. A second sensitivity regressed score change on remission, baseline score and concurrent inflammatory-score change. The latter describes shared longitudinal variation and was not interpreted as a controlled direct effect because inflammation resolution may be part of the same recovery process. No pooled cross-platform effect was estimated because deposited scales, drugs, follow-up windows and endpoints differed. Analyses used R 4.3.3 and Python 3.12.10; scripts, dependency locks, provenance records and result tables are available at the project repository.

## Results

### Cohort assembly and score coverage

All six candidate genes were measured after canonical annotation on each microarray platform. The common inflammatory covariate contained the same 194 genes in GSE92415, GSE23597 and GSE73661, preventing platform-specific gene-set membership from changing the adjusted estimand. The final bulk analyses included 65, 32, 23, 27 and 13 paired patients in the discovery, primary validation, infliximab-healing, vedolizumab-week-6 and vedolizumab-week-12 analyses, respectively (Table 1).

### Exploratory clinical-response association did not reproduce in the primary validation

In GSE92415, responders showed a larger six-gene score increase in the exploratory model adjusted for arm, baseline score and inflammatory change (beta=0.599, 95% CI 0.148 to 1.051; P=0.010; Figure 2A). The response coefficient remained positive after additional adjustment for 11 components of a published metabolism-and-response-to-stress program (beta=0.373, 95% CI 0.074 to 0.672; P=0.015), but this attenuation was descriptive and did not establish an independent biological mechanism. Omitting the candidate baseline reduced the estimate to 0.375 (95% CI -0.115 to 0.866; P=0.131), showing that the discovery association depended on the conditional model.

The locked GSE23597 primary validation did not meet its support rule. The response coefficient was 0.401 (95% CI -0.481 to 1.282; P=0.359; n=32), and the mandatory no-baseline analysis was also imprecise (beta=0.267, 95% CI -0.744 to 1.277; P=0.592). HC3 inference, the infliximab-only subset and all leave-one-patient-out fits did not convert the result into a supported validation.

### The score tracked infliximab-associated endoscopic healing

In the GSE73661 infliximab cohort, patients with endoscopic healing had a larger score increase after adjustment for baseline score and inflammatory change (beta=1.195, 95% CI 0.503 to 1.887; P=0.00185; n=23, including 8 healed patients; Figure 2). HC3 inference gave a similar interval, and all 23 leave-one-patient-out coefficients were positive (range 1.074 to 1.346). The observation with the largest Cook's distance was a non-healed patient with a large positive score change; its removal increased rather than created the healing coefficient.

The association closely tracked the endoscopic phenotype. All eight healed patients had positive changes between 1.181 and 1.814, and the score change correlated with continuous endoscopic change (Spearman rho=-0.593; P=0.0029). This provides a molecular description of mucosal repair, while also indicating partial construct overlap: a healed surface contains more mature absorptive epithelium by definition. Published CT-colonocyte, JAK/STAT and oxidative-phosphorylation proxies were also associated with healing in parallel models, so the data did not establish superiority of the six-gene score or independence from every inflammatory program.

### Vedolizumab estimates were directionally concordant but inconclusive

The vedolizumab estimates were positive at week 6 (beta=0.521, 95% CI -0.435 to 1.478; P=0.271; n=27, 6 healed) and week 12 (beta=0.853, 95% CI -0.385 to 2.091; P=0.154; n=13, 3 healed), but both intervals crossed zero (Figure 2A). The week-6 interval also included the infliximab point estimate of 1.195. These data neither established a shared cross-drug effect nor demonstrated a difference between anti-integrin and anti-TNF therapy.

### Remitters showed a larger CT-colonocyte score rebound from a lower baseline

In the adalimumab single-cell cohort, the CT-colonocyte proportion within non-ileal epithelium did not increase preferentially in remission (beta=-0.188, 95% CI -0.756 to 0.379; exact P=0.490; n=19; Figure 3A). Among the 16 patients eligible for the CT-state score, remitters had a lower baseline mean than non-remitters (6.166 versus 8.209; difference=-2.042; Welch P=0.015), whereas follow-up means were similar (7.935 versus 7.421; difference=0.514; P=0.426; Figure 3B). The unadjusted Post-minus-Pre difference was 2.556 (95% CI 1.267 to 3.845; exact P=0.00050; two-axis q=0.0010; Figure 3C). Baseline score and score change were strongly inversely correlated (r=-0.775; P=0.00042).

Baseline-adjusted ANCOVA attenuated the remission coefficient to 1.442 (HC3 95% CI 0.121 to 2.764; P=0.0347). The corresponding v3.1-only estimate was 1.577 (95% CI -0.765 to 3.918; P=0.164; n=13), and the released author-paired subset estimate was 1.151 (95% CI -1.320 to 3.623; P=0.327; n=14). The raw contrast therefore represented a larger recovery from a lower baseline; the smaller subsets did not estimate the adjusted association precisely.

Candidate-score change correlated inversely with concurrent inflammatory-score change (r=-0.594; P=0.015). In a descriptive model including remission, baseline candidate score and inflammatory-score change, the remission coefficient was 1.340 (HC3 95% CI -0.819 to 3.498; P=0.201). This model did not isolate an inflammation-independent effect because inflammatory resolution may be part of the same recovery process.

Among the 16 jointly evaluable patients, the Kitagawa composition component was -0.141 (95% CI -0.534 to 0.251; exact P=0.464), whereas the within-group expression component was 1.483 (95% CI 0.361 to 2.606; exact P=0.0156; Figure 4). The total epithelial change difference was 1.342 (95% CI 0.047 to 2.636; exact P=0.0447). The group difference was therefore arithmetically carried by expression changes within CT and other epithelial groupings rather than by an increased captured CT fraction.

### The corrected epithelial-state family supported a CT-restricted signal

After DecontX correction using released labels, the CT-colonocyte estimate was 2.854 (95% CI 1.259 to 4.449; exact P=0.00112; q=0.0169 across the fixed 15-state family). Transit-amplifying and LGR5-positive stem estimates remained nominally positive but did not survive the family correction (both q=0.140). We therefore retained the CT-state association and withdrew a broader cross-lineage recovery claim. Because cluster-informed DecontX preferentially preserves genes typical of the labelled CT state, the larger corrected coefficient was not interpreted as stronger evidence. The PTPRC and COL1A1 negative controls changed in the opposite direction to the candidate score and did not pass their fixed two-gene correction; patient-level contamination-change estimates were positive but imprecise. These checks argue against a uniform ambient-RNA rise while leaving gene-specific ambient effects and label dependence unresolved.

### Complete external-analysis ledger

Table 2 lists every external outcome analysis performed for the fixed six-gene score. One of five analyses was the original primary clinical-response validation and was negative; two produced supported positive associations in distinct outcome and data contexts; and the two vedolizumab analyses were directionally positive but inconclusive. The ledger prevents the positive endoscopic and single-cell findings from being presented as replacements for the failed primary test.

## Discussion

Across longitudinal public cohorts, the six-gene score captured recovery of mature absorptive transcription during UC mucosal healing. In the adalimumab single-cell cohort, remitters showed a larger score rebound within author-labelled CT colonocytes, while the captured CT fraction did not increase; algebraic decomposition likewise assigned the larger group difference to expression changes within CT and other epithelial groupings. The signal therefore contained information beyond counting more CT colonocytes in the biopsy. Its magnitude was also shaped by a lower baseline score in future remitters, and follow-up score levels did not clearly separate the groups.

The bulk results define where this component is clinically visible and where it is not yet reproducible. Infliximab-associated endoscopic healing showed a clear association, consistent with restoration of absorptive transport and epithelial metabolic functions represented by AQP8, CA2, SLC26A3, GUCA2A, HMGCS2 and MS4A12. The primary clinical-response validation in GSE23597 was negative, however, and the vedolizumab estimates were too imprecise to establish cross-drug generality. Clinical response includes symptoms and physician assessment, whereas endoscopic healing directly measures the tissue compartment represented by the score. The combined evidence supports use of the score as a mucosal tissue-state readout and does not support its use as a stand-alone predictor of clinical response.

The biological content of the score is deliberately conservative. Mature colonocyte depletion, inflammatory epithelial remodeling and reconstitution during healing have been described by single-cell and organoid studies [3,4]. Several transcriptomic programs already stratify disease activity or therapeutic outcome [10-12]. The six-gene set was formed post hoc after broad discovery review, shared four genes with a published CT-colonocyte panel and tracked that panel closely in GSE92415. The increment is therefore not six new genes or a new epithelial population. It is the longitudinal, patient-level separation of a familiar mature-colonocyte signal into captured composition and expression within an author-labelled state, while retaining a failed primary clinical-response validation.

Several features reduce common public-data artefacts. Gene aliases were harmonized before validation so that platform age did not silently alter score membership. The inflammatory adjustment used the same 194 genes in all microarray cohorts. Patients, rather than biopsies or cells, were the inferential units. No cross-platform pooled P value was calculated. Influence analyses showed that the infliximab association was not created by one patient. In the single-cell analysis, the state family and a result-contingent interpretation rule were fixed before DecontX correction; only the CT association survived that family correction. Negative-control transcripts did not reproduce the candidate direction.

The study has material limits. The candidate set was assembled after examining the discovery cohort, so the discovery P value is descriptive and does not account for the full candidate-generation search. The primary external clinical-response test was negative. Bulk endoscopic healing and mature surface-epithelial expression partly measure the same repaired mucosa, and the single-cell clinical-remission cohort cannot resolve that construct overlap for the infliximab samples. In GSE282122, all remitters were profiled with 10x v3.1, all v3 patients were non-remitters, remitters started with lower candidate scores and candidate recovery coincided with inflammation reduction. Baseline-adjusted estimates were positive but imprecise in the v3.1-only and author-paired subsets. The 20-cell threshold excluded three non-remission patients. Published-label DecontX has limited ability to remove genes typical of the labelled state, so its CT result is label-dependent. Sampling location, follow-up timing, dose and other treatment differences could not be fully adjusted. Public microarray scales differed, and no cross-platform effect was pooled.

A practical use of this result is to separate two treatment readouts that are often collapsed in bulk data. CT-cell abundance and CT-cell mature identity can be quantified independently, allowing future studies to ask whether a therapy restores epithelial function within residual colonocytes before, after or without a measurable change in cell composition. The six genes provide a low-dimensional assay target for that state component. Prospective testing with paired endoscopy, histology and expression, followed by targeted RNA or protein measurement within epithelial cells, would be required before clinical deployment.

## Conclusions

The six-gene score measures recovery of mature absorptive transcription in treated UC mucosa. Remitters showed a larger rebound within author-labelled CT colonocytes that was not explained by a greater captured CT fraction, while lower baseline values, concurrent inflammation resolution and technical imbalance contributed uncertainty to the remission contrast. The score tracked infliximab-associated endoscopic healing, whereas the internally specified clinical-response validation was negative and vedolizumab estimates were inconclusive. It is a molecular tissue-state readout of mucosal repair, not a validated response predictor or independent mechanism.
